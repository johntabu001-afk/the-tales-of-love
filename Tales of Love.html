Tales from the Heart


David & Tatiana Love Story

Under the golden acacia canopy of Nairobi’s City Park, I first laid eyes on Tatiana. It was a crisp Saturday morning fifteen years ago. She was a university student, hopelessly attempting to sketch the park's famous monkeys. Being a young architect with an easy smile and a thermos of sweet chai, I offered to help. Over shared laughs, mandazis, and stolen glances, a delicate spark flared between us. Our friendship quickly bloomed into romance. I wanted to show Tatiana my roots in the rolling, tea-blanketed hills of Kericho. On our first big road trip together, we journeyed west, passing through the lush landscapes of Kiambu and descending the breath-taking Great Rift Valley. We stopped in Naivasha to watch the flamingos and enjoy hot fish by the lake. Under the setting sun, I softly took her hand and promised to love her endlessly. Tatiana, who hailed from the coastal beauty of Mombasa, brought me to her home the following year. Walking along the white sands of Nyali Beach at dusk, with the warm Indian Ocean breeze whispering through the palm trees, she showed me the rich warmth of coastal hospitality and swahili coffee. Standing at the shore as the tide rolled in, I whispered to her that every journey across Kenya only led me deeper into her heart. our love was built on simple, beautiful Kenyan moments: late-night phone calls during sudden Nairobi rainstorms, weekend drives up to the chilly peaks of Nyeri, and long getaways through the wildlife-rich savannahs of Maasai Mara in Narok County. Through every career milestone and personal hurdle, we were each other’s steadfast anchor. When the time came to ask her to be my wife, I took Tatiana to the serene, misty edges of Mount Kenya in Nanyuki. Beneath a blanket of stars in the quiet splendor of Laikipia County, I got down on one knee. With tears of joy glistening in her eyes, she said yes before I could even finish asking. Our traditional Ruracio and wed ding were a vibrant celebration of culture, unity, and deep affection. Family and friends gathered from Kakamega to Machakos, filling the air with traditional songs, colorful fabrics, and heartfelt blessings. Now, fifteen years later, Tatiana and I sit on our veranda overlooking the serene hills of Machakos County, where we built our family home. Holding her hand just as tightly as I did that first day in City Park, we watch our children play in the yard. Through fifteen years, countless counties crossed, and a lifetime of shared memories, our love remains as sweet, warm, and enduring as the Kenyan sun.


Mary & Erick Romance

It was twenty years ago during the vibrant Mombasa Cultural Festival along the Indian Ocean coast when I first saw Mary. She was coordinating an art exhibition near the historic Fort Jesus, completely lost in thought as she arranged hand-woven coastal tapestries. I was a young civil engineer working on a port expansion project in Kilifi County, and I offered to help her hang a heavy wooden frame. When our hands brushed against the carved wood, Mary looked up with a warm, radiant smile that instantly anchored me to her. Our romance blossomed along the coastline. We spent our early weekends sipping spiced kahwa in Old Town Mombasa, taking long evening strolls along the white sands of Diani Beach in Kwale County, and sharing endless conversations about our dreams under the tropical stars. As the years went by, our journey took us across the country. I took Mary to my home county of Meru, nestled along the fertile slopes of Mount Kenya. Standing together among the fragrant coffee and tea plantations, with the cool mountain air surrounding us, I held Mary close and promised her a lifetime of devotion. Mary brought that same coastal warmth to the chilly highlands, making every place we went feel instantly like home. When I decided to ask Mary to be my wife, I planned a trip to the vast wilderness of Taita Taveta County. Standing at a magnificent viewpoint overlooking Tsavo as the sun dipped behind the hills, casting golden hues across the acacia trees, I got down on one knee. With tears of happiness in her eyes, she whispered a tearful, enthusiastic "Yes!" as the African wilderness stood witness. Our wedding was a joyous fusion of cultures, bringing together loved ones from the Coast to the Central highlands. Drums echoed, songs filled the air, and our families celebrated a union built on unconditional love and respect. Now, twenty years later, Mary and I sit together on our quiet balcony in Kajiado County, overlooking the wide plains where wildlife gently grazes in the distance. As we watch our teenage children laugh together in the garden, I reach for Mary's hand. Twenty years, multiple counties, and a lifetime of precious memories later, she is still my greatest blessing and my forever home.
